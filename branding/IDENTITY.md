# Mihrab — identity

The one place Mihrab's identity is written down. Every store listing, the
website, the README, the GitHub pages and the release notes are written
from this file. When a surface disagrees with it, the surface is wrong —
or this file is, and it changes first.

## What Mihrab is

> **Mihrab is a Muslim's daily companion: the prayers, the Qur'an, and the
> remembrance in between — in one calm, private app, on every device you
> own.**

Mihrab began as a prayer-times app. It has not been one for a long time,
and nothing public should describe it as one. Prayer times are where the
day starts, not the whole of what the app is: it is where you read and
listen to the Qur'an, make dua and dhikr, keep your fasts and your prayers,
and it does all of it without an account and without knowing who you are.

### The name

A *mihrab* is the niche in a mosque's wall that shows which way the prayer
faces. The app does the same for a day: it points you to the next prayer,
and keeps the Qur'an, dua and dhikr within reach between them.

## The four pillars

Every description of the app is organised around these, in this order.
A new feature belongs to one of them; if it belongs to none, that is worth
noticing before it ships.

| | Pillar | Svenska | العربية | What is in it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pray** | Be | صلِّ | Prayer times (on-device, a provider, or the published national tables for Sweden and Morocco), adhan, reminders, silencing the phone at prayer time (Android), qibla, the pinned countdown, widgets, logging each prayer with its journal, streak and graph. |
| 2 | **Read and listen** | Läs och lyssna | اقرأ واستمع | The Madinah muṣḥaf drawn as text from the King Fahd Complex page fonts, four riwāyāt, tajweed colours on Ḥafṣ and Warsh, the word reader, 42 reciters, Tilāwah, 13 translations and tafsir, memorisation tools, khatmah plans. |
| 3 | **Remember** | Minns | اذكر | 100+ duas from Ḥiṣn al-Muslim, tasbih, the fasting log, the Hijri calendar, the ayah of the day. |
| 4 | **Yours** | Ditt eget | لك وحدك | No ads, analytics, tracking or account. Works offline. Sync between your own devices with no account and no server. Backup to a file. Open source (AGPL-3.0). iPhone, iPad, Android and Mac, 13 languages. |

The fourth pillar is the reason to *choose* Mihrab, not the reason to
*want* it. It closes every description; it does not open one.

## What sets it apart

Lead with these — they are what make someone switch from the app they
already have. State them as facts about Mihrab, never as "the only app
that…": nobody can check every other app, and both stores reject
superlatives they cannot verify.

- **The word reader** — hold any word in the muṣḥaf and hear that one word read.
- **Tajweed colours on Ḥafṣ *and* Warsh**, every rule explained, in 13 languages.
- **Four riwāyāt**, the printed page drawn as text: sharp at any size.
- **Tilāwah** — surah into surah with the screen off, the recited word lit on the page.
- **Sync with no account and no server** — sealed files in a folder you already sync.
- **Open source on every platform**, iPhone and Mac included; on F-Droid without Google.
- **National timetables where they exist** (Sweden, Morocco) and the Maliki second times.

## Voice

- **Calm.** The app is opened five times a day, in moments that matter.
  Nothing in it should raise the pulse: no exclamation marks, no emojis,
  no countdown urgency in marketing copy.
- **Precise.** Say what it does. "Hold a word to hear it read", not
  "powerful audio features". A number when there is one: 42 reciters,
  17 adhan recordings, 13 languages.
- **Humble.** No "best", "#1", "ultimate", "revolutionary", "all-in-one",
  "super app". The work speaks.
- **Respectful.** ﷺ after the Prophet's name where the text names him.
  Religious terms spelled with care, and explained rather than assumed.
- **Without guilt.** Never "never miss a prayer again", never anything
  that casts the reader as failing. The journal records; it does not scold.
- **Privacy as a fact, not a fear.** "No account, and nothing leaves your
  phone that you did not ask to send" — not "Big Tech is watching you".

## Words

| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| companion | prayer times app (as what it *is*), tool, aid, utility, super app, all-in-one |
| the prayers, the day's prayers | salah reminders, prayer alarm app |
| the Qur'an (prose), Quran (store titles, search) | Koran (in English) |
| muṣḥaf (app, site, README) · mushaf (stores) | Quran reader, Quran viewer |
| remembrance, dua, dhikr, tasbih | Islamic content |
| open source, AGPL | "free" as the headline — it is free; lead with what it does |
| on your phone, on your own devices | in the cloud, your account |

**Spelling.** Anything a search engine or a store indexes as a title or a
short description uses plain Latin letters — *Quran, mushaf, riwayat,
Tilawah, Hafs* — because that is what people type. The app, the website's
body and the README use the transliteration — *Qur'an, muṣḥaf, riwāyāt,
Tilāwah, Ḥafṣ*.

**Swedish** uses *Koranen*, *bönetider*, *böneutrop*, *dua/duor*, *tasbih*,
*tajwid*, *Medinamushafen*. **Arabic** uses *القرآن الكريم* on first
mention and *القرآن* after, *المصحف المدني*, *الأذكار*, *التجويد*.

would reject an upload.

## Titles and search

Play weighs the title above every other field, and the store-listing test
was written when the title carried *prayer times* for exactly that reason.
The title now carries the descriptor instead, because it is the one line
everyone reads; the search words move to the short description, the App
Store subtitle and keywords, and the first line of every description,
where they are still indexed:

- short description: *prayer times · adhan · Quran · dua · qibla*
- App Store subtitle: *Prayer Times, Quran & Qibla*

Watch Play Console → Grow → Store performance → Search for a few weeks
after a title change. If search installs fall in one language, that
language's title can carry its search term again (`Mihrab: Namaz Vakti`)
without changing anything else here.

## When something changes

- **A new feature** goes into its pillar in the full descriptions and the
  README before the release that ships it — the release notes say what is
  new, the descriptions say what the app *is*.
- **A feature that sets Mihrab apart** also goes into the list above, and
  earns its place in the first paragraph of the descriptions.
- **A new language** means a new row in the title table and a new store
  directory; the store-listing test says so.
- **This file changes first.** Then the surfaces.
