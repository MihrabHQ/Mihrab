# The transliterations, checked against the Arabic

The Latin-letter editions under an ayah (`src/quran/transliteration.ts`) come
from outside: three generated by rule (risan/quran-json), one written by a
person (Muhammet Abay's Turkish). These are the working scripts that checked
every ayah of each against the bundled Arabic on 2026-11-24, kept so the
check can be run again when a mirror is rebuilt. They are rough, and they are
not part of the app or of `npm run preflight`.

## What they do

- `tr.py` reads the Arabic (Tanzil's Uthmani text, Ḥafṣ) into the scholarly
  Latin spelling by the rules of connected reading: the article and the sun
  letters, hamzat al-waṣl, tanwīn and nūn sākinah before each letter
  (idghām, iqlāb), the long vowels and their shortening before a waṣl, the
  pronoun's ṣilah, tā' marbūṭah, the opening letters, and the pausal form at
  a stop. It was written here, from the Arabic, without the source's code.
- `check.py` compares an edition to it, ayah by ayah, letter for letter.
  The edition marks its breaths with commas and they need not be where the
  Arabic has a pause mark, so every way of breaking the ayah is tried.
- `respelt_simple.py`, `respelt_id.py` show that the plain English and the
  Indonesian editions are the scholarly one in another spelling, so one
  check covers the three.
- `check_tr.py`, `abay_read.py` put Abay's Turkish into the same spelling
  and list where it differs; `abay-2026-11-24.txt` is that list.
- `fixes.py` holds the corrections and proves each against the Arabic and
  across the three spellings (`src/quran/transliterationCorrections.ts` is
  written from it).

## Running it

    mkdir work && python3 tools/transliteration-check/prep.py work
    # the four editions, 114 files each, from this repository's releases
    for e in translit.en translit.en-simple translit.id-skb tr.transliteration; do
      gh release download data-translation-$e -R MihrabHQ/Mihrab -D work/$e
    done
    cd work && cp ../tools/transliteration-check/*.py .
    python3 check.py 40 hamza     # the scholarly edition against the Arabic
    python3 respelt_simple.py && python3 respelt_id.py
    python3 check_tr.py && python3 abay_read.py
    python3 fixes.py

## What it found (2026-11-24)

**risan/quran-json, `en`:** 6,215 of 6,236 ayahs identical to the second
derivation; the 21 others read by hand.

- 10 where a word is wrong: 7:196, 25:49, 46:33, 75:40 (a syllable of
  waliyyiya / yuḥyiya dropped), 21:88 (nunjī written nujī), 27:36 (ātāniya
  written ātāni), 21:71 (a stray hamza), 26:14 and 81:9 (dhambin written
  dhambum, dhambim), 59:7 (fantahū cut to fantah).
- 4 where the word is right and its join is not the one written everywhere
  else: 2:96, 3:30, 74:40 (tanwīn before w, y), 3:112 (n before b).
- 1 left as it is: 52:37, al-musayṭirūn with s. Ḥafṣ is read both ways.
- The rest are the second derivation's own limits, and the edition is
  right: 2:72, 11:41 (imālah), 41:44 (tashīl), and where a hamza is written
  (10:51, 10:91, 17:21, 41:11, 93:4).

Two of the ten (21:88, 27:36) were wrong in the edition **and** in the
second derivation — a small nūn and a small yā' with a vowel that it did not
know. Abay's text, which is independent of both, showed them; after that
every mark that occurs fewer than 400 times in the text was listed and each
of its places read.

`en-simple` is `en` respelt in 6,235 ayahs (the other, 11:41, writes the
imālah "majrayhaa"); `id-skb` is `en` respelt in every ayah.

**Abay's Turkish:** 5,900 ayahs identical to the derivation once respelt.
Of the 336 others:

- 185 differ only in where he pauses or in how a pause is written (hû for
  huwa, the ending kept or dropped): the same words.
- 15 are the same word in another spelling, or the derivation's limit, or
  he is right (2:72, 3:37, 6:19, 7:189, 7:196, 10:89, 21:88, 25:49, 27:22,
  32:7, 41:44, 45:32, 46:4, 46:33, 75:40).
- 23 write a length the script shows and the reading does not (anā, ūlī,
  lākinnā).
- **113 have a wrong letter, a wrong length or a wrong word**: an emphatic
  for a plain letter or the reverse (muḥsanāt for muḥṣanāt, liyaqdiya for
  liyaqḍiya), an article not assimilated (lildīni, falnāru), a singular for
  Ḥafṣ's plural (sayyi'atu, shahādatihim) and, in sūrahs 29 and 30
  especially, words that are not the ayah's (29:13, 29:46). They are in
  `abay-2026-11-24.txt`.

His text is not corrected in the app: it is another author's work, and
what to do with it is the maintainer's decision (docs/data-sources.md).

## What this is not

A program checked a program, and a second text checked both. No ḥāfiẓ or
qāri' has read these lines. The check is of letters: it cannot hear.
